## 1. Purpose and the problem being fixed

The engine takes research inputs and produces reports. Two sample outputs showed why v2 is needed:

1. A Kiro brief cited a 2023 paper on general AI assistants for a Kiro-specific productivity figure ("up to 35%"). Only one of eight papers was actually about Kiro. The brief presented an evidence gap as if it were an answer.
2. The brief grouped weak-venue and strong-venue sources together with no grading, propagated a corrupted term ("vibrate coding" for "vibe coding"), and filled content ideas by pasting the raw topic string into templates.
3. A second sample report had headline numbers (20x, 67%, 90%) with no citations, decorative stock images captioned as figures, and invented framework acronyms.

**Goal of v2:** a question-driven pipeline whose outputs are traceable, honestly graded, and reviewed by a human before anything is published. The owner is a senior delivery leader building public thought-leadership on AI governance in multi-vendor enterprise delivery. His credibility depends on every claim being checkable.

**North-star behaviours:**
- Every factual sentence traces to a graded claim in a ledger.
- The system says what is NOT known as clearly as what is.
- A human decides what is signal. The engine drafts evidence; it never writes the owner's opinions or first-person experience.
- Local-first, low cost, no confidential data leaving the machine.

## 2. Hard constraints (non-negotiable)

1. **Local-first.** Ollama for extraction and triage. Free public APIs for papers. A stronger remote model may be used ONLY for synthesis over public-lane claims, behind a config flag that defaults OFF, via a provider abstraction. The practitioner lane must never reach any remote model.
2. **Confidentiality.** Practitioner data lives in a separate SQLite file (`practitioner.db`), gitignored, never exported except through the Gate 6 abstraction check. A gitignored `denylist.txt` (client, vendor, programme and person names) is scanned before any publish.
3. **Copyright-safe outputs.** Store metadata plus a short source excerpt (max 25 words) per claim for verification. Outputs paraphrase. Any direct quote is under 15 words, at most one per source, clearly marked. No verbatim run of 8 or more words from a source outside a marked quote.
4. **Source terms.** Respect robots.txt and each site's terms. No scraping paywalled content. Use official APIs (arXiv, Semantic Scholar, Crossref, OpenAlex, SEC EDGAR with a proper User-Agent). Cache responses; rate-limit politely; retry with backoff.
5. **State durability (mandatory from day one).** A schema_version table, forward-only migrations, an automatic timestamped backup before every migration and before bulk writes (keep the last N), a migration test that loads a populated old-version DB and checks nothing is lost, SQLite WAL mode, and an integrity check on startup. Do not ship any schema change without its migration and test.
6. **No rebuilds.** Do not rewrite working fetchers, the CLI, or the local frontend. Extend. Keep the existing CLI (`--topic`, `--mode quick|deep`) working; old outputs are marked `legacy-ungraded` and can never be published.
7. **No invented branding.** Outputs must not introduce acronyms or named frameworks unless a cited source uses them. No stock imagery.
8. **Determinism.** Seeds, model IDs, config and code version are recorded per run so a run can be reproduced.

## 6. The three lanes

**A. Academic.** Keep existing fetchers. **C. Practitioner.** Entry through the local frontend (Section 9, View 7). Structured fields: situation, decision, action, outcome, metric if any, sector, date, abstraction confirmed. Stored only in `practitioner.db`. Grade defaults to `anecdote`; `observations_count` records repeat sightings and never lifts the grade on its own. Nothing here is exported before Gate 6.

## 7. Claim extraction and grading

**Extraction.** Local model, structured JSON output, schema-validated, one claim per atomic assertion. Record `extraction_confidence`. Reject and log malformed output; never silently repair it. Normalise terms against the source text so a term of art cannot be corrupted (the "vibrate coding" failure): extracted key terms must appear in the source excerpt or be flagged.

**Entity matching.** `exact` only if the source is actually about the entity named in the claim. A general AI-assistant paper never supports a Kiro-specific sentence; it can be `topic_level` only.

**Grading is rule-based and deterministic** (`grade/rules.yaml`, thresholds in config):
- `strong`: verified peer-reviewed venue + empirical study type + entity match adequate for the claim + no unresolved contradiction, OR independently measured by 2 or more independent sources.
- `moderate`: reputable preprint or peer-reviewed with empirical method but narrow context, OR 2 or more independent customer claims.
- `weak`: position paper, conceptual framework, unverified venue, or abstract-only extraction with no method detail.
- `vendor_claim`: source org has a commercial stake (vendor blog, marketing, sponsored research), unless independently corroborated.
- `anecdote`: single case, practitioner lane, or social post.
- **Caps:** abstract-only caps at moderate; an unresolved contradiction caps at weak; entity mismatch means the claim is usable only at topic level.
- Independence: sources from the same organisation or author group count once.
- Store `grade_reasons_json` listing each rule that fired so the frontend can show why.

## 8. Gap matrix and synthesis

**Gap matrix.** Per question, state each lane's state (supported | partial | none found | contradicted) and assign an outcome 1 to 4. Outcomes 2, 3 and 4 are the strategic value; surface them prominently. "None found" requires a matching `search_log` entry.

**Synthesis.**
- Reads claims only (approved, or auto-graded if the Gate 3 policy allows).
- Every assertive sentence carries claim IDs.
- Strength-verb mapping in `strength_verbs.yaml`: strong = shows/demonstrates/finds; moderate = suggests/indicates; weak = reports/proposes/one study claims; vendor_claim = "[org] states"; anecdote = "in one case". No sentence may exceed the cap of its weakest cited claim.
- Opinion and field-experience sentences (`type = opinion|field`) are written by the human in the frontend. The engine never generates them and never writes first-person experience.
- Failing sentences are flagged or removed. Never silently rewrite a failure into a pass.
- No template that interpolates the raw topic string into prose. Lint for repeated boilerplate sentences.

**Output templates.**
1. *Field Note*: Question; What the evidence supports; What it does not support; The gap (matrix outcome); My view [human]; Evidence appendix linking graded claims.
2. *Strategy document*: Vision; Evidence-graded current state; Gaps; Bets; 12-month plan; Kill criteria per bet; Assumptions to test.
3. *JSON export* for the website (versioned schema).

## 10. Scoring the final output

Thresholds and weights live in `score/scoring.yaml` so the owner can tune them. The scorer runs after every draft and on demand in the frontend, and stores `score_json` and `gates_json` on the output.

**Definitions.** *Assertive sentences* = sentences making factual claims (excludes headings, questions, `opinion`, `field`). A claim is *eligible* if approved or auto-graded under the active Gate 3 policy.

### 10.1 Hard gates (any failure = status BLOCKED; the score is shown but marked invalid)

| Gate | Rule |
|---|---|
| G1 Traceability | 100% of assertive sentences cite at least one eligible claim ID |
| G2 Numeric fidelity | Every numeral (percentages, multipliers, currency, counts) in an assertive sentence appears in a cited claim's value or text. Derived numbers must be tagged DERIVED with formula and input claim IDs |
| G3 Entity fidelity | If a sentence names a product, company or standard, every cited claim has `entity_match = exact` (or `family` where the sentence says so) for it |
| G4 Language honesty | No sentence's strength verb exceeds the cap of its weakest cited claim |
| G5 Confidentiality | Zero denylist hits and zero practitioner content not passed through abstraction |
| G6 Copyright | No quote of 15+ words, no more than one quote per source, no verbatim run of 8+ words outside marked quotes |

### 10.2 Quality score (0 to 100, meaningful only if all gates pass)

| Component | Weight | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| S1 Evidence strength | 30 | Mean over assertive sentences of min(1, grade_value(sentence) / best_grade_available_for_question). grade_value: strong 1.0, moderate 0.7, weak 0.4, vendor_claim 0.3, anecdote 0.2 (use the weakest cited claim). Normalising to the best available evidence means honest reporting of a thin literature is not penalised. |
| S2 Corroboration and diversity | 15 | 0.7 x (share of key findings supported by 2 or more independent sources) + 0.3 x (share spanning 2 or more lanes) |
| S3 Question coverage | 15 | Share of (question, lane) cells with at least one eligible claim or an explicit "searched, nothing found" record backed by `search_log` |
| S4 Gap disclosure | 10 | Share of in-scope questions whose gap-matrix outcome is stated in the output, averaged with recall of ledger contradictions that the output mentions |
| S5 Freshness and scope | 10 | Share of cited claims inside the question's time window (defaults: enterprise 24 months, academic 36 months; human can mark a source foundational) |
| S6 Human review | 10 | 0.6 x (share of cited claims reviewed at Gate 3) + 0.4 x (share of Gates 1 to 6 completed) |
| S7 Reproducibility | 10 | Re-run extraction and grading from cached sources; score = agreement (Jaccard of approved claim set, weighted by grade agreement) |

**Bands:** 85+ publish-ready (still passes Gate 6 by hand); 70 to 84 revise; below 70 do not publish. BLOCKED overrides any band.

**Diagnostics (tracked, not scored, to avoid perverse incentives):** engine-draft versus human-final edit distance; share of sentences flagged and removed; claims rejected at Gate 3 by reason.

### 10.3 Engine-level quality (separate from per-output score)

- Extraction precision and recall against a human-labelled sample (start with 50 claims from the existing Kiro papers). Starting target: precision 0.90 or higher; report recall.
- Grading agreement between the rules and human grades on that sample. Starting target: 0.80 or higher; every disagreement becomes a rule review.
- Regression suite pass rate must be 100%.
- Starting thresholds are proposals; tune them from real data and record changes in `DECISIONS.md`.

### 10.4 Frontend scorecard

Show gates (pass/fail with the offending sentences), the seven components with their inputs, the band, and links to the sentences and claims that lowered each component.
